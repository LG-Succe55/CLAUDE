The seven initiatives · In depth

Strategy 01

Scale Proven Creative Enablement
1

AI-Supported Visualization & Concept Development

STAGE A
Why

Real effort stands between a strong idea and shared understanding — refining, rendering, placing it in an environment, animating, and building the presentation.

What changes
  • More alternatives tested before commitment
  • Earlier, clearer communication of ideas
  • Less time preparing reviews
  • Leadership compares directions earlier
Scope / where it starts

Concept rendering, sketch-to-image, color & material exploration, contextual visualization, animation, presentation & review prep — with governance and measurement.

2

Workflow-Based AI Training & Adoption

STAGE A
Why

Access to a tool does not change work. People need to know where AI applies, how to use it on a real assignment, what to protect, and how to judge output quality.

What changes
  • Approved capabilities used in core workflows
  • Role-based learning paths
  • AI Champion network for peer learning
  • Responsible use becomes routine
Scope / where it starts

Role-based paths, discipline-specific training, AI Champions, prompt & workflow libraries, coaching, adoption dashboards, and continuous improvement.

The seven initiatives · In depth

Strategy 02

Develop Intelligence in Critical Workflows
3

Design Knowledge Capture & Retrieval

STAGE B
Why

Design creates valuable knowledge in reviews, decisions, critiques and historical programs — yet the reasoning behind decisions is often fragmented or lost.

What changes
  • Reliable, source-linked answers
  • Faster review preparation
  • Continuity between program phases
  • Preserved rationale; faster onboarding
Scope / where it starts

Start with a focused pilot on Design-review information for one program. Discovery defines sources, classification, traceability, ownership, access, and platform fit.

4

AI Vehicle Packaging Intelligence

STAGE B
Why

Packaging sets the physical framework Design works within. Important constraints can become visible only after a direction has already advanced.

What changes
  • Comparable packages found quickly
  • Constraints understood earlier
  • Alternatives compared efficiently
  • Conflicts caught before major investment
Scope / where it starts

An assistant for H-Point & package-library search, architecture comparison, constraint explanation, dimensional alternatives, and basic conflict identification.

5

3D Surface Geometry Automation

STAGE B
Why

Geometry development blends craftsmanship with repetitive technical work that consumes time expert sculptors could spend on visible Design quality.

What changes
  • Less manual effort on defined tasks
  • Faster iteration after package changes
  • Earlier geometry-error identification
  • More expert time on Design quality
Scope / where it starts

Bounded tasks — shut-face, B-side geometry, surface adaptation & morphing, foam-block prep, geometry checking. Discovery gauges feasibility and value.

Strategy 03

Build Connected Design Intelligence
6

Design-to-BOM & Cost-of-Change Intelligence

STAGE C
Why

A visible Design change can ripple into parts, engineering, tooling, suppliers, weight, validation, timing and cost — consequences not immediately visible to Design.

What changes
  • Better-informed trade-offs
  • Fewer late surprises; reduced rework
  • Earlier cost awareness
  • Creative intent aligned to business reality
Scope / where it starts

A limited first feature or subsystem, connecting Design geometry, product definitions, engineering, BOM, suppliers, tooling, cost, timing, and historical changes.

7

Connected Design Intelligence & Optimization

STAGE C–D
Why

Knowledge and product information are distributed across systems, regions and functions — rarely accessible in the context of an actual Design decision.

7A · Design Intelligence Fabric

The governed connective layer linking reviews & decisions, historical programs, package, geometry, materials, engineering, BOM & cost, suppliers, timing, and benchmarks — so many AI products can use consistent, traceable, protected information.

7B · Closed-Loop Optimization

Uses the fabric to give earlier feedback on alternatives — evaluated against package, aerodynamics, weight, cost, manufacturing, materials, and regulation. It informs consequences; the team still decides.

Operating model · Hub Leader accountabilities

Four accountabilities, end to end

Leadership positioning

We are clear about the future we are building and the strategic areas that matter. We are equally clear that several opportunities require structured discovery before the organization commits to a final solution or investment.

1

Scale where evidence exists

Expand what already works into repeatable, measurable capability.

2

Discover with discipline

Define the problem, data, and solution where the opportunity is strong but unproven.

3

Build the foundation incrementally

Add trusted data and connected capability as validated use cases become available.

4

Sharpen as evidence grows

An intentional model that protects investment, accelerates learning, and directs resources.

This is not uncertainty without direction. It is an intentional transformation model — and the roadmap becomes more precise as the team produces better evidence.
